Rooftop Solar System Cost in India 2026: State-Wise Price Guide

Direct Cost Answer: In 2026, the gross turnkey cost of installing a grid-connected residential rooftop solar system in India ranges from ₹55,000 to ₹72,000 per kW before government incentives. Under the central government’s PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana, eligible individual residential households receive a direct financial subsidy of ₹30,000 for 1 kW, ₹60,000 for 2 kW, and a fixed cap of ₹78,000 for systems 3 kW and larger. Consequently, the net consumer investment for an average 3 kW system drops to ₹87,000 – ₹1,32,000, generating 360–420 electricity units per month and achieving simple financial payback within 2.8 to 3.8 years.

India’s residential and commercial rooftop solar market has undergone a historic transformation following the nationwide rollout of the PM Surya Ghar initiative. With retail electricity tariffs rising across state DISCOMs and modern high-efficiency N-type TOPCon and Mono PERC solar modules entering mainstream deployment, rooftop solar is no longer merely an ecological choice—it is one of the highest-yielding capital investments available to Indian property owners.

However, real-world installation costs vary considerably depending on system architecture (on-grid, off-grid, or hybrid), panel wattage, mounting structure elevations (standard flat roof vs. elevated habitable superstructures), local DISCOM net-metering fees, and state-specific subsidies. This comprehensive 2026 price guide breaks down turnkey system costs across capacities (1 kW to 100 kW), audits all 28 states and 8 union territories, dissects component-level bills of materials, explains usable roof-space formulas, and provides an interactive savings calculator to help you make an informed solar decision.

What is Included in the Solar Installation Price?

A comprehensive turnkey rooftop solar quotation covers far more than just the solar panels. In professional solar engineering, a complete grid-tied installation encompasses 11 critical mechanical, electrical, and regulatory sub-systems. Understanding this itemized Bill of Materials (BOM) prevents consumers from falling prey to unscrupulous contractors who quote deceptively low prices by omitting essential safety devices such as lightning protection, dual chemical earthing, or certified DC isolators.

1. Solar PV Modules (42% – 48%)

High-efficiency Mono PERC or N-type TOPCon modules (540Wp – 590Wp) with 16-BB or 10-BB half-cut cell technology. Must be DCR-compliant (Domestic Content Requirement) and listed on the MNRE ALMM (Approved List of Models & Manufacturers) to claim PM Surya Ghar subsidy. 25–30 year linear performance warranty.

2. Grid-Tied Solar Inverter (16% – 20%)

Pure sine-wave string inverter with dual Maximum Power Point Trackers (MPPT), integrated anti-islanding protection (compliant with IEEE 1547 / IEC 62109), IP65 weather rating, Wi-Fi data-logging dongle for smartphone monitoring, and minimum 5 to 10 year manufacturer warranty.

3. Mounting Structure (MMS) (10% – 13%)

Hot-Dip Galvanized (HDG) steel with minimum 80-micron zinc coating or anodized extruded aluminum (6063-T6). Engineered to withstand wind loads of 150 to 180 km/h. Fasteners must be Stainless Steel grade SS304 to prevent electrolytic corrosion over 25 years.

4. DC & AC Solar Cables (5% – 7%)

Cross-linked polyethylene (XLPO) insulated, UV-resistant, halogen-free, flame-retardant 4mm² or 6mm² copper DC solar cables (EN 50618 compliant). Multi-strand armored AC copper/aluminum cables for inverter-to-grid connection with less than 1.5% voltage drop.

5. ACDB & DCDB Distribution Boxes (3% – 5%)

IP65 polycarbonate enclosures housing 1000V DC fuses, DC isolator switches, Type II Surge Protection Devices (SPDs) to arrest atmospheric over-voltages, and Miniature Circuit Breakers (MCBs) / Residual Current Circuit Breakers (RCCBs) on the AC output.

6. Earthing System & Lightning Arrester (4% – 6%)

Mandatory 3 dedicated maintenance-free chemical earthing pits with copper-bonded electrodes (1 for Inverter AC, 1 for Module frame & structure, 1 for Lightning Arrester) with earth resistance below 5 Ohms. Class I/II copper lightning arrester mounted at the highest rooftop elevation.

7. Balance of System (BOS) (2% – 3%)

Genuine IP68 Stäubli MC4 connectors, rigid UV-stabilized PVC conduits or galvanized cable trays, stainless steel cable ties, warning signboards, civil foundation anchoring, and chemical fastener grouting.

8. Installation, Engineering & DISCOM Liaisoning (7% – 10%)

Professional civil and electrical installation labor, structural shade analysis, single-line diagram (SLD) preparation, DISCOM net-metering application processing, joint inspection coordination, and plant commissioning.

Why Does an On-Grid Solar Inverter Shut Down During a Power Cut?

Grid-tied solar inverters are legally required by the Central Electricity Authority (CEA) and international safety standards (IEEE 1547 / IEC 62109) to feature automatic Anti-Islanding Protection. If grid power fails, the inverter disconnects within 2 seconds. This prevents lethal high-voltage solar current from back-feeding into the local utility transmission lines while DISCOM linemen are performing maintenance. If uninterrupted power backup is essential for your home, you must invest in either a dedicated home inverter/UPS battery bank alongside your on-grid solar or opt for an engineered Hybrid Solar System with an integrated Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS).

How Much Roof Space is Required for Solar Panels?

A common misconception is that required roof space equals the cumulative surface area of the solar panels. In practical solar engineering, the actual usable roof area needed is 25% to 40% greater than the physical footprint of the modules. Proper layout design must account for inter-row spacing (pitch) to prevent winter solstice inter-row shading, parapet wall shadows, water tank clearance, and maintenance access corridors.

The Engineering of Inter-Row Pitch & Shadow Avoidance

Modern commercial 540Wp – 590Wp solar modules measure approximately 2.278 meters in height by 1.134 meters in width, yielding a physical area of 2.58 m² (~27.8 sq.ft) per module. When mounted at an optimal tilt angle equal to local latitude (typically 12° to 28° across India, facing True South), each row casts an elongated shadow northward during the winter solstice (December 21st, solar noon).

Row Pitch = L × cos(Tilt Angle) + L × [sin(Tilt Angle) / tan(Solar Altitude Angle at 21 Dec Solar Noon)]

In Mumbai or Pune (Latitude ~18.5°N, Tilt ~15°), each row requires at least 0.8m to 1.2m of clear inter-row gap. In North Indian cities like Delhi or Jaipur (Latitude ~27°N–28°N, Tilt ~25°), inter-row clearance expands to 1.4m to 1.8m to eliminate 9:00 AM to 3:00 PM mutual row shading.

Engineering Deratings: Why Systems Generate Less Than Rated Nameplate Capacity

Real-world solar PV plant performance is subject to several inevitable environmental and electrical losses:

  • Thermal Coefficient Loss (-6% to -12%): Solar cells lose voltage as temperatures climb. Standard modules possess a temperature coefficient of -0.34%/°C above 25°C. On hot Indian summer days when rooftop module surfaces reach 60°C, peak power drops by approximately 11.9%.
  • Soiling & Dust Losses (-3% to -8%): Particulate dust, vehicular emissions, and bird droppings obstruct sunlight. In dry urban centers (Delhi, Jaipur, Ahmedabad), uncleaned panels can lose 10%–15% efficiency within 3 weeks without washing.
  • Inverter Conversion Loss (-2% to -3%): Leading string inverters achieve 97.5%–98.5% European efficiency, dissipating 1.5%–2.5% of DC energy as heat.
  • Ohmic Cable Drop (-1% to -1.5%): Resistance in undersized DC or AC copper cables causes power loss. Professional design limits voltage drop to <1.5%.
  • Annual Degradation (-0.45%/year): High-grade TOPCon modules degrade ~1.0% in Year 1 and 0.40%–0.45% annually thereafter, guaranteeing >87.4% performance at Year 30.

Key Factors Influencing Rooftop Solar Costs in India

When comparing contractor quotations, property owners frequently encounter price discrepancies of ₹15,000 to ₹35,000 for the same nominal system size. These variations are driven by fundamental differences in component quality, structural engineering, electrical protection, and statutory compliance.

1. Module Cell Technology (TOPCon vs Mono PERC)

Older Polycrystalline panels are virtually obsolete in 2026. Standard P-type Mono PERC panels (21% efficiency) represent the budget baseline. N-type TOPCon panels (22.5%+ efficiency, lower temperature loss, and superior bifaciality) command a 5%–8% price premium but produce 4%–6% more energy annually.

2. DCR Mandate & ALMM Listing

Modules manufactured using Indian-made cells and wafers (DCR) carry a domestic manufacturing premium of ₹2 to ₹4 per watt over imported Chinese Tier-1 panels. However, DCR compliance is legally non-negotiable for PM Surya Ghar subsidy eligibility.

3. Structure Height & Material

Standard short-leg HDG structures (0.5m–1.5m clearance) cost ₹6,000–₹7,000/kW. If you elevate the structure to 8–10 feet high to retain usable terrace area for walking or recreational use, structural steel weight triples, adding ₹12,000 to ₹18,000/kW.

4. Inverter Brand & Architecture

Single-MPPT Chinese budget string inverters cost significantly less than dual-MPPT European or Tier-1 Asian brands (Sungrow, Solis, Growatt). Micro-inverter architectures (Enphase) provide module-level MPPT and fire safety but increase overall system cost by 35%–45%.

5. DC/AC Cable Run Distances

Standard quotations factor in 30 meters of DC cable and 15 meters of AC cable. If your roof is 4 floors above the main electrical distribution panel, extra heavy-duty 6mm² or 10mm² armored cable and masonry chasing add ₹8,000 to ₹15,000 to the bill.

6. Number of Dedicated Earthing Pits

Low-cost installers cut corners by installing only 1 earthing pit or connecting solar to existing building ground. CEA safety standards mandate 3 independent chemical pits with bentonite compound, adding ₹8,000–₹12,000 in material and civil boring labor.

Maintenance and Long-Term Ownership Expenses

Because solar PV systems possess zero moving mechanical parts, operational and maintenance (O&M) expenses are negligible compared to diesel generators or conventional HVAC machinery. However, neglecting periodic cleaning and electrical inspections will degrade system efficiency by 12% to 25% over time.

1. Module Cleaning Protocols

Frequency: Every 10 to 14 days in dry/dusty conditions; monthly during winter.
Water Quality: Use low-TDS water (<100 ppm) to prevent calcification scale formation on glass. Never use abrasive chemical detergents.
Thermal Shock Prevention: Clean panels exclusively in the early morning (before 8:00 AM) or evening (after 5:30 PM). Splashing cold water on solar glass heated to 60°C under mid-day sun causes micro-cracking and voids warranties!

2. Electrical Inspections & Torque Checks

Annual preventative maintenance should include infrared thermal camera scans of junction boxes and modules to detect high-resistance hot spots. Technicians must check torque on MC4 connectors, test AC/DC SPD health indicator windows, and measure earthing electrode resistance to ensure ground impedance remains below 5 Ohms.

3. Inverter Mid-Life Replacement Reserve

While solar modules come with 25 to 30 year warranties, string inverters contain electrolytic capacitors and power semiconductors with an operational design life of 10 to 12 years. Prudent property owners should budget a replacement reserve of approximately ₹30,000 to ₹45,000 around Year 10–12 for inverter overhaul or replacement.

Understanding Solar Warranties: Don't Confuse the Three Types:

  • Product Warranty (10 to 12 Years): Covers physical manufacturing defects, frame delamination, junction box failures, and ingress leaks.
  • Performance Warranty (25 to 30 Years): Guarantees minimum power output (typically at least 90% at Year 10 and 84%–87.4% at Year 25/30).
  • EPC Workmanship Warranty (1 to 5 Years): Provided by your local installer covering roof leakages, structural fastener integrity, and electrical termination faults.

Installers assemble the galvanized structure, chemically anchor base plates, mount the solar modules, run UV-resistant DC cabling in conduits, connect ACDB/DCDB boxes with SPDs, drill 3 chemical earthing pits, and mount the lightning arrester.

Upload your bank account details and cancelled cheque on the portal. The Ministry of New & Renewable Energy (MNRE) verifies the commissioning certificate and disburses the central subsidy directly into your bank account via DBT.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) — Solar Cost in India 2026

1. What is the total cost of a rooftop solar system in India in 2026?

In 2026, the gross turnkey cost of an on-grid residential rooftop solar system in India ranges from ₹55,000 to ₹72,000 per kW before government subsidies. For an average 3 kW residential home, the gross turnkey cost is approximately ₹1,65,000 to ₹2,10,000. After deducting the maximum central PM Surya Ghar subsidy of ₹78,000, the effective net investment paid by the homeowner is ₹87,000 to ₹1,32,000.

2. What is the price of a 1 kW solar system before and after subsidy?

A 1 kW grid-tied solar system typically costs ₹60,000 to ₹75,000 before subsidy. Under PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana, a 1 kW residential system qualifies for a direct central subsidy of ₹30,000, bringing the net consumer cost down to ₹30,000 to ₹45,000. It requires ~80–100 sq.ft of shadow-free roof area and generates 120–140 electricity units per month.

3. What is the price of a 3 kW solar system in 2026?

A standard 3 kW residential solar system costs approximately ₹1,65,000 to ₹2,10,000 gross. Because 3 kW is the threshold that captures the full central subsidy cap of ₹78,000 (₹30,000/kW for the first 2 kW + ₹18,000 for the 3rd kW), the net homeowner investment is ₹87,000 to ₹1,32,000. In Uttar Pradesh, an additional state subsidy of ₹30,000 brings the net cost down to under ₹80,000!

4. What is the price of a 5 kW solar system?

A 5 kW grid-tied solar system costs ₹2,55,000 to ₹3,20,000 before subsidy. The central PM Surya Ghar subsidy remains capped at ₹78,000 for all capacities 3 kW and higher, resulting in a net cost of ₹1,77,000 to ₹2,42,000. A 5 kW system generates approximately 600–700 units monthly and requires 400–500 sq.ft of roof space.

5. How much does a 10 kW rooftop solar system cost?

A 10 kW rooftop solar installation costs between ₹4,80,000 and ₹5,90,000 gross. If installed for an individual residential home, it receives the standard capped subsidy of ₹78,000 (net cost: ₹4,02,000 to ₹5,12,000). If installed by a commercial entity, factory, or hospital, it receives no direct residential cash subsidy but qualifies for 40% Accelerated Depreciation tax savings and GST input tax credit.

6. How much subsidy is available under PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana?

Under PM Surya Ghar, the central subsidy is structured in fixed slabs: ₹30,000 for 1 kW, ₹60,000 for 2 kW, and ₹78,000 for 3 kW and above for individual residential connections. Group Housing Societies and Resident Welfare Associations (RWAs) qualify for ₹18,000 per kW up to a maximum capacity of 500 kW for common utility loads.

7. Is rooftop solar really free under PM Surya Ghar?

No. While the scheme is titled ‘Muft Bijli Yojana’, the installation itself is not free. The homeowner must pay the net capital cost after subsidy (typically ₹38,000 for 1 kW, ₹68,000 for 2 kW, or ₹1,07,000 for 3 kW). The term ‘free electricity’ refers to the fact that once installed, the system generates up to 300 units of electricity per month with zero ongoing fuel cost, eliminating that corresponding consumption from your DISCOM bill.

8. Does every household qualify for the solar subsidy?

Central residential subsidies are restricted to domestic individual households with an active residential electricity consumer account and suitable independent rooftop space. Commercial businesses, industrial complexes, agricultural connections, and government offices are ineligible for direct PM Surya Ghar capital subsidies.

9. How much roof space is required for a 3 kW solar system?

A 3 kW solar installation using modern 550Wp modules consists of 6 panels, occupying a physical panel area of ~167 sq.ft. However, on a flat RCC terrace, the practical usable roof area required is 240 to 300 sq.ft to accommodate inter-row pitch spacing, parapet wall shadow clearance, and walking paths for cleaning.

10. How many units does 1 kW of solar generate per day in India?

In India, 1 kW of solar PV generates an average of 4.0 to 5.2 kWh (units) per day, depending on regional solar irradiation, tilt angle, and module cleanliness. Annually, 1 kW generates between 1,400 and 1,650 units of electricity.

11. Is a battery storage system necessary for rooftop solar?

No. In an On-Grid solar system, no batteries are required. Excess solar energy generated during the daytime is exported directly into the utility grid through a bi-directional net meter. Batteries are only necessary if you opt for an Off-Grid or Hybrid system to provide backup during frequent grid blackouts.

12. Does an on-grid solar system work during a power cut?

No. Standard grid-tied solar inverters automatically shut down within 2 seconds of a grid failure. This is a mandatory safety mechanism known as Anti-Islanding Protection (IEEE 1547 / CEA regulations), designed to prevent lethal solar current from flowing into grid wires while linesmen are working. To run solar during blackouts, you need a Hybrid Inverter with an automatic transfer switch or a separate battery backup.

13. How long does a rooftop solar system take to recover its investment?

For residential on-grid systems utilizing the PM Surya Ghar subsidy, the simple financial payback period ranges from 2.8 to 3.8 years. For commercial consumers paying high tariffs of ₹10–₹12/unit and claiming Accelerated Depreciation, payback is often reached in 2.0 to 2.8 years.

14. Does the solar installation price include net metering?

Professional EPC turnkey quotations generally include the liaisoning, paperwork, single-line diagrams, and DISCOM inspection coordination. However, some utilities charge a direct statutory fee for bi-directional meter testing, application processing, or meter security (typically ₹2,000 to ₹6,000), which may be billed directly on your monthly electricity bill.

15. How do I choose a reliable, empanelled solar installer?

Verify that the installer is officially empanelled on the National Portal (pmsuryaghar.gov.in). Ensure they provide written confirmation of DCR-compliant ALMM modules, Hot-Dip Galvanized structures with at least 80 microns zinc coating, 3 dedicated chemical earthing pits, and a minimum 5-year workmanship warranty.

16. Which Indian states provide extra subsidies on top of PM Surya Ghar?

Uttar Pradesh provides the most generous state top-up: ₹15,000/kW up to a maximum of ₹30,000 on residential installations. Delhi provides a Generation Based Incentive (GBI) of ₹3/kWh for 5 years on systems up to 3 kW. Himachal Pradesh provides approximately ₹4,000/kW state assistance. Other states provide expedited single-window net-metering approvals without direct cash top-ups.

17. How much does a rooftop solar system cost in Maharashtra?

In Maharashtra (MSEDCL, Tata Power, Adani Electricity), a 3 kW system costs approximately ₹1,70,000 to ₹2,05,000 gross. After deducting the ₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy, the net consumer outlay is ₹92,000 to ₹1,27,000. High residential tariff slabs in Mumbai and Pune make payback exceptionally rapid (~3.0 to 3.4 years).

18. Can rooftop solar be installed in apartments and housing societies?

Yes. Group Housing Societies (GHS) and Resident Welfare Associations (RWAs) can install rooftop solar on common building terraces under PM Surya Ghar, receiving a central subsidy of ₹18,000 per kW up to 500 kW. The generated power is used to offset common community electricity bills (elevators, water pumping, corridor lighting, and EV charging points).

19. What maintenance does a rooftop solar system require?

Rooftop solar requires minimal maintenance: wash the glass modules every 10 to 14 days using low-TDS water during early mornings or evenings. Annually, an electrical technician should inspect cable terminations, verify inverter error logs, check SPD surge status, and measure earthing resistance to ensure it remains below 5 Ohms.

20. How do I calculate the exact solar capacity needed for my house?

Divide your average monthly electricity consumption (units recorded on your bill) by 130 (the average monthly generation of 1 kW in India). For example, if your family consumes 390 units per month: 390 ÷ 130 = 3.0 kW. Alternatively, you can use our interactive calculator in Section 11 above to estimate your recommended capacity automatically.
